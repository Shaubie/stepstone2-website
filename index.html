About

I'm a senior studying data science at the University of Wisconsin–Madison.

Most of what I enjoy about the field happens before the model does: getting messy, inconsistent, real-world data into a shape where it can actually answer a question. Statistics and machine learning are the tools, but the interesting work is deciding what's worth asking.

Outside of coursework I build small projects, read, and spend more time than I should looking at paintings.

A painting I keep coming back to

Picasso painted it within weeks of the bombing of Gernika in April 1937. There is no color in it at all — only greys, blacks, and a bruised white — and it is still the loudest painting I have ever stood in front of. Every figure is mid-scream or mid-collapse, and the whole thing is lit by a single bare bulb, as if the scene were being documented rather than composed.
